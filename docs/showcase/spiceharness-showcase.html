SpiceHarness

A tool that turns a circuit question into several candidate designs, simulates every one in ngspice under the same frozen test bench, ranks them by a fixed rule, and re-simulates the winner from scratch before calling it a pass.

Anirudh Sankar Krishnan · Python 3.13 · ngspice-47 · vendor SPICE models (LM741, LM358, OPA1641, 2N3904/3906, 1N4148)

The idea

The language model proposes. The simulator decides.

A language model is good at suggesting topologies and explaining tradeoffs, but its numbers are guesses. Hand simulation gives trustworthy numbers for one circuit at a time. SpiceHarness splits the work: the model (Claude Code here) may only submit structured circuit descriptions. It cannot change the test bench, supply its own measurements, or swap a real device model for an ideal one.

harness1 · FreezeSources, loads, probes, analyses and pass/fail bounds are fixed before any candidate is seen.
model2 · ProposeCandidate circuits as typed component lists from a checksum-pinned catalog.
ngspice3 · MeasureOperating point, AC and transient runs; gain, cutoff, THD, tone amplitude, noise.
harness4 · RankPassing beats failing, then fewer parts. Closest miss ranks first among failures.
ngspice5 · Re-verifyThe winner is simulated again independently. Only verified and feasible counts.

Robustness

Does it still pass with real part tolerances?

A nominal pass is not a design. A Monte Carlo study perturbs every candidate-owned R, C and L within its tolerance, re-runs ngspice for each sample, and counts how many still meet every bound. Draws are seeded and deterministic, so the same study always gives the same samples. Results are statistical estimates, not worst-case proofs.

Why the numbers can be trusted

What the harness enforces

  • The bench is fingerprinted. A candidate cannot change sources, loads, probes, analyses or bounds.
  • Device models are vendor files with pinned checksums. Ideal substitutions are refused.
  • Circuits that would mislead ngspice are rejected before simulation: a net named gnd, voltage-source or inductor loops, non-finite samples.
  • The winner is re-simulated independently. A run is only reported as solved when that rerun is both verified and feasible.
  • Every candidate exports to an editable KiCad schematic and the original ngspice deck, so results can be checked outside the tool. Edited schematics can be imported back and re-scored as new candidates.
  • 340 automated tests. The Monte Carlo, viewer and KiCad code was audited by a separate AI reviewer and every confirmed defect fixed.

Known limits

  • Results are the best found in a bounded search (20 evaluations, 180 s), not a proof of the global optimum.
  • Tolerance studies vary passives only. Op-amp offset, GBW spread and temperature are not varied yet.
  • Injected supply noise is not the same as internal device noise or calibrated PSRR.
  • No switching converters, PCB parasitics or arbitrary SPICE devices.
Where I would value your advice

Open questions

  1. Is the ranking rule sensible?

    Today a passing circuit with fewer parts always wins, and ties fall to submission order. Above, the LC filter has far more margin and identical part count but ranks second. Should margin, tolerance yield, or cost enter the ranking, and in what order?

  2. Is the tolerance model realistic enough?

    Independent, symmetric draws: R ±1 %, C ±5 %, L ±10 %, uniform by default. Would you add op-amp parameter spread (offset, GBW), correlated tolerances within a batch, or temperature first?

  3. A Lab 4 result I could not reproduce

    For the supply-noise version of this circuit, I understood the intended answer to be a decoupling capacitor from the point between the two noise generators to ground. In my simulation that capacitor removes the DC reference for both rails, and the bias collapses. Per-rail decoupling to ground passes every bound. I suspect my model of the noise generators differs from the Multisim part (I assumed a 1 Ω source; the Multisim noise part may be a 10 MΩ thermal-noise resistor). Where is my model wrong?

Reproduce it 
spice experiment compare --spec examples/experiments/interference-filter/spec.json \
    --candidates examples/experiments/interference-filter
spice experiment candidates RUN_ID          # leaderboard
spice experiment montecarlo RUN_ID -n 200   # tolerance study of the winner
spice view RUN_ID                           # browser view of every candidate
spice export RUN_ID                         # KiCad schematics + ngspice decks
